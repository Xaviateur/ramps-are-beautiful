The Vision

Why Ramps Matter

A ramp is not merely a technical solution. It is a statement about who belongs in the world. The ramp, in its most radical form, is architecture that says: "You are expected here. You are ordinary. Your presence is normal."

This project documents the beauty, history, and politics of the ramp—as told through photographs, diagrams, stories, and the work of Michael Daniels and the Center for Independent Living.

Ramps: A Brief Genealogy
  1. 1972The Center for Independent Living is founded in Berkeley by disabled activists.
  2. 1986Michael Daniels publishes Ramps are Beautiful: The Architecture of Independence with the CIL.
  3. 1990The Americans with Disabilities Act is signed; 1:12 becomes the national maximum gradient.
  4. 2026The book's spirit returns as an open, bilingual archive of ramps worldwide.

A global collection of ramps documented through photographs, diagrams, and design analysis.

How to Design and Build a Ramp

  1. Step 1: Understand the grade. ADA requires maximum 1:12 (1 inch of rise per 12 inches of run). Shallower is better.
  2. Step 2: Calculate landing spaces. Every 30 inches of rise requires a flat landing of at least 5 feet.
  3. Step 3: Add handrails. Both sides, graspable diameter, continuous along entire run.
  4. Step 4: Choose durable materials. Consider weather, wear, maintenance, and aesthetic intention.

Materials & Aesthetics

The ramp is not infrastructure. It is architecture. Choose materials that integrate with the building's character: wood for warmth, steel for clarity, concrete for boldness. A ramp can be invisible—or it can be proud.
